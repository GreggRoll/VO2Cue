# Heart Sync Preview — 1.2 (8)

This preview lives on `heart-sync`, with a dedicated internal TestFlight group named **Heart Sync Testing**. It includes an embedded Apple Watch app. Install build 8 on both devices; this is a testing build, not an App Store submission.

## Start here

1. Open **Settings → Heart-rate targets**. Enter and save your maximum BPM, or estimate it from age using 220 − (0.7 × age). The estimate is rounded, editable and explicitly confirmed; age is not stored. New-user onboarding includes this setup, and existing users without a maximum receive it before their next Watch start. Custom BPM ranges remain available in the workout editor. No maximum is guessed from Apple Health. Personal maximum changes take effect on the next workout.
2. Open **Settings → Heart Sync Preview → Add 6½-minute Heart Sync Check**. This adds an editable profile: 30-second warmup, two 2-minute work intervals, 90-second recovery, and 30-second cooldown. The button never starts exercise automatically.
3. Edit the profile if needed, then tap **Start with Apple Watch**. Open VO2Cue on the paired Watch and allow workout and heart-rate access when asked. A slow permission response can expire the start request; simply start again. A failed phone acknowledgement can mean Watch already started, so check Watch before retrying.
4. Alternatively, start a saved profile on Watch and open the iPhone app to view the same workout. Normal **Start workout** remains a phone timer for profiles without guidance; an explicit Watch start is also available. **Use phone timer without heart rate** retains timing without a sensor.

Default targets are 85–95% of the user-confirmed maximum during work and 60–70% during active recovery, with editable per-profile BPM overrides. These are training targets from [NTNU CERG](https://www.ntnu.edu/cerg/advice), not medical thresholds. Profiles without an explicit guidance setting automatically receive targets for Watch workouts after a maximum is saved. Explicitly disabled guidance remains disabled. Phone-only timers retain their existing start behavior. Warmup and cooldown show BPM without deviation coaching. Existing custom phase labels remain intact.

## Physical-device checks

- Compare phase, round, remaining time, raw BPM, and the target on both devices. The shared phone/Watch target meter, range status and halfway cues use the current rounded BPM displayed on screen. Below, in, and above classifications use the same inclusive lower/upper limits in hard and recovery phases.
- Start from each device. Pause, resume, skip and end from each device. Phone commands wait for Watch acknowledgement, are deduplicated, expire after eight seconds, and cannot skip a different phase than the one selected. The phone never starts a competing interval engine for a Watch workout.
- Work below target shows **Building** during the first 90 seconds. Recovery above target shows **Settling** during the first 60 seconds. Above-target work remains visible; below-target recovery says **Below target**. The removed easy-recovery and gradual-building coaching text and trend explanation are no longer shown. These transition windows are product parameters for evaluation.
- Coaching requires ten seconds of sustained deviation and a fresh new sample, with a 45-second cooldown. Interval cues take priority. Pause and stale data suppress coaching; at hard/recovery halfway points, Heart rate up/down/good replaces Halfway using the fresh displayed BPM. Recovery below range now says Heart rate up; good is reserved for readings within the inclusive target limits. Missing samples, disabled HR cues, and warmup/cooldown retain the normal Halfway cue. Heart-rate cues obey the per-profile toggle; speech also requires Spoken cues.
- Lock iPhone, lower your Watch wrist and background the phone app. Enable Live Activities for VO2Cue before locked-phone testing. Check the phase timer on the Live Activity and reopen the app to compare current state. The Live Activity displays the interval timer, not a continuously refreshed heart-rate readout.
- Move the phone out of range and return. Watch should continue independently. Phone controls become unavailable during a stale connection. On reconnection, the full current state replaces the phone projection. Phone projection freezes after three seconds without an update rather than executing independent phase changes.
- Remove the Watch or otherwise create a sensor gap. After 15 seconds without a fresh reading, BPM and comparison disappear; the timer continues. Waiting, paused and missing readings are distinct. No cached Apple Health reading is used as current HR. Empty data does not establish denied read permission.
- Test swimming and vigorous wrist movement. Do not expect a fixed sampling cadence or guaranteed subsecond radio delivery. Sync diagnostics show measurement age, update age and estimated delivery delay using device clocks; they do not measure sensor latency.
- Finish normally and end early. Confirm one local history entry per session ID, correct active/paused/skipped time, and a single Apple Health workout saved by Watch. The iPhone mirror never saves another Health workout. Check completion after disconnect/reconnect as well.
- If Watch restarts during an active session, HealthKit recovery restores the saved interval state and remirrors it with a newer revision. Check recovery timing on real devices; the checkpoint is refreshed at transitions and about every ten seconds.

## Implementation and privacy

Watch owns the `HKWorkoutSession`, `HKLiveWorkoutBuilder`, interval runtime and coaching classifier. iPhone registers `workoutSessionMirroringStartHandler` at initialization. An expiring acknowledged WatchConnectivity message supplies the exact profile after `startWatchApp` wakes Watch; workout telemetry and controls use HealthKit's bidirectional mirrored-session channel. Profiles, confirmed maximum settings, purchase access and completed records retain durable WatchConnectivity delivery.

Live packets preserve fractional-second sample timestamps, session IDs, phase IDs and ordered revisions. Sends have a three-second completion timeout, coalesce routine state updates, and reserve bandwidth for acknowledgements and final state within HealthKit's 100 KB/10-second limit. Completed records prevent older packets from reopening a workout. Timers, settings and existing saved records remain backward compatible.

Health information and diagnostics remain on the paired devices. Heart-rate graphs and the session target snapshot are stored in local workout history on both devices; none is sent to the developer. Graphs use at most 480 compact points across the whole workout and reduce resolution as needed. Pauses and skips break line continuity; sensor gaps longer than 15 seconds also break the line. Coordinates use planned workout time with pauses excluded, leaving skipped remainders blank. Routine packets include only the latest point; full graph snapshots are sent about every ten seconds for reconnection/backfill, plus checkpoints and completion. TestFlight has no simulated heart-rate source. Debug-only, clearly labeled UI fixtures are compiled out of Release builds.

The live timeline and saved workout detail show hard/recovery cycle bands, dashed upper/lower target lines, and the measured BPM line only after actual Watch heart-rate points have been recorded. Phone timers, histories without readings, and Watch sessions awaiting a first sample have no timeline. Pausing or temporarily losing signal keeps previously recorded points available. On Watch the phase name uses the inline navigation title, reducing the gap above the countdown and freeing room for the target text. On Watch, tap the heart-rate readout to open the timeline; tap Timer to return. The timeline is also a separate workout page. On iPhone, tap the graph to inspect recorded readings. No interpolated value is reported for selections more than 15 seconds from a recorded point. There is no time-in-zone score.
